Critical Inspection Stages

Before closing up, inspect at the points where you can still stop, fix, and move forward.

01 Structure & Concrete Pours

Checking workmanship before the concrete is closed, including honeycombing, rebar cover, concrete integrity, cracks, and overall pour quality. A problem caught at this stage costs a few hours of fixing. A problem that moves to the next stage can cost tens, if not hundreds of thousands of baht in demolition and rebuilding.

02 Waterproofing

Checking critical points before the work gets covered up. A waterproofing issue that isn't caught in time can surface within days, weeks, or months, requiring expensive work that would have been entirely avoidable if caught early.

03 Electrical & Plumbing

Checking that utilities, locations, and workmanship match the plan you agreed on with your contractor, before the walls are closed. After the walls are closed, the same problem becomes a repair job that wasn't in your budget, or a compromise you have to live with.

04 Finishes & Handover

Identifying defects and workmanship issues before you sign off on handover. Once you accept the property, the contractor is gone, and every repair becomes your expense.

Serious Structural Finding

Multiple Defects in a Concrete Column (Deviation, Honeycombing, Cracks)

This finding revealed several problems at once. The orange line in the photo shows the deviation between the column's axis and true vertical. A column that's off by even one millimeter at the base can be off by centimeters at the height of the next floor. The beam poured on top of it will carry uneven loads, create unplanned stresses, and become a silent failure point that only shows up when it's too late. Honeycombing in the concrete body, along with cracking and chipping, are dangerous problems caused by improper concrete pouring and handling.

What was done on site: Detailed measurement results, a stop-work order to the contractor, and a safety requirement to fix every column before pouring the beams.
Zoom in
⛔ Work Stopped On Site

Rebar Bent Against the Structural Engineer's Instructions

The workers bent the moment-resisting rebar on their own, without engineer approval, just to make it fit the formwork more easily. It's a shortcut that only looks harmless, but rebar bent in the field outside its designed flex points loses its ability to transfer moment force, creates stress concentration points, and endangers the integrity of the beam for the life of the building, and the safety of the people living in it.

What was done on site: Work was stopped immediately. The contractor was required to inspect and replace all defective rebar.
Zoom in
⛔ Work Stopped On Site

Pipes Embedded Without a Protective Sleeve Before Plastering

The contractor was moments away from closing up the plaster. The pipes had been embedded directly in the brickwork without a protective sleeve, and as a result were completely crushed, with grooves running the full length of the pipe. This is a very common practice in Thailand, and it's completely wrong. If the work hadn't been stopped in time, any future repair would have required tearing everything apart just to find the source of a leak.

What was done on site: Work was stopped. The contractor was required to replace the pipes and install protective sleeves. Once the plaster is on, there's no way to know what's hidden inside.
